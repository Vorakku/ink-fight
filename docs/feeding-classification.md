# Who eats enemies in Inkfight

Classification reviewed against the current roster on 2026-11-14. This is a game
rule informed by diets and folklore, not a claim that adults grow infinitely in
nature. No game checks, builds or browser verification have been run.

## Rules

Eating uses the existing Noodle rule: an eligible creature's own enemy kill is a
meal. Only final deaths count; phoenix eggs and cockroach recovery poses do not.
A meal consumes the body, so the same remains cannot also become cocoons, an ant
delivery or a dung ball. Existing collectors may still use other fallen bodies.
No new corpse-hunting AI is introduced.

Generic meals add 5% of original body scale and 10% of original maximum health
and attack damage, without a gameplay cap, and heal to the new maximum. Noodle
keeps its 10% scale, extra tail segment and +30 maximum health per meal, and also
gains the damage bonus. Only Noodle crowns itself and summons little snakes.
Adult dragons are the size-growth exception: meals still increase health and
damage, but adult body size increases only on completed evolution, in small
stage increments. Egg-born hatchlings first mature with time and meals, and
cannot evolve until they reach full adult size.
Meal counts are separate from each creature's existing ability counters.

Edible victims are biological fighters and Food. Buildings, the Bugmobile,
gargoyles, zombies, vampires and temporary copies are not meals. Hedgehogs,
mantises and antlions use a conservative Bug-only prey rule; other enabled eaters
can consume the broader edible set. These prey limits are gameplay choices,
not exhaustive species-specific diets. Unattributed environmental deaths do not
give anyone a meal. Growth from named attacks also applies to ranged attacks.
Burns, propagated fire and stink clouds retain a living source's generation for
credit; the source's death or conversion prevents credit going to a replacement.

## Mythical

There is no scientific diet to verify for invented creatures. Historical texts
support some choices; the others below explicitly describe our interpretation.

| Fighter | Enemy eating | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Scorch / dragon | Yes | Choose a flesh-eating folklore dragon; devouring prey occurs in recorded tales [27]. Evolves at 3/8/15/25 meals, rests in a cave and lays eggs as a rainbow boss |
| Encore / phoenix | No | Several classical accounts describe non-animal nourishment, including sunlight; retain rebirth/healing identity [28] |
| Pointy / unicorn | No | Horse-like magical grazer is our game interpretation, not a universal folklore diet |
| Sparklebutt / Pegasus | No | Winged-horse grazer is our game interpretation |
| Special Delivery / griffin | Yes | Predator interpretation inferred from its eagle/lion anatomy; not a single universal historical diet [29] |
| Horsing Around / centaur | No | Armed half-human fighter, not inherently an enemy-devouring monster; game choice |
| Giant | No by default | Generic giant is not automatically an ogre or cannibal; no such identity is specified |
| Red Light / gargoyle | No | Stone/architectural construct interpretation [30] |
| Two For One / hydra | Yes | Serpentine predator interpretation; explicitly a game choice |
| Good Boys / Cerberus | Yes | Hesiod describes raw-flesh eating [31] |
| Under Bridge / troll | Yes | The bridge troll threatens to eat the goats in the recorded tale [32] |
| Monthly Subscription / werewolf | In wolf form | Predatory wolf transformation is supported by Lycaon traditions; human form does not feed on enemies [33] |

Water, Hero, People, Food and Miscellaneous fighters do not gain enemy eating
through their category. Any later extension should assign eligibility explicitly.
